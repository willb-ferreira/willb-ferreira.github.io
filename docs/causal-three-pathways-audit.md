# Causal Inference — three introductory pathways and bibliographic audit

Date: 2026-09-29. Scope: only the public `causal` guide and research-card summary/keywords, plus links to the five previously established guides. The current six-area site structure, stable `#causal` anchor, research status (“Developing research interest”), personal publications, students, courses and the full original causal bibliography are preserved. The original, more detailed guide was read before this rewrite and remains in `assets/research-library.js`.

## Scientific decisions

The original guide correctly distinguished **causal identification** from **statistical estimation**, covered potential outcomes and structural causal models, and curated ten works spanning propensity scores, instrumental variables, marginal structural models, modern difference-in-differences, heterogeneous treatment effects and double/debiased machine learning. Its public renderer, however, showed only three generic references and did not organize this content into the three short, linked pathways established elsewhere on the site. This revision does not introduce a new umbrella research area or assert completed causal-research publications.

The public pathways are:

1. **Causal identification and research design / Identificação causal e delineamento de estudos.** Define intervention, units, target population, causal estimand and design. Study potential outcomes, DAGs, consistency, exchangeability, positivity, suitable adjustment, experiments and observational identification. Association or prediction by itself is not causal identification. Some assumptions, notably absence of unmeasured confounding, cannot be conclusively verified from observed data alone.
2. **Semiparametric estimation and causal machine learning / Estimação semiparamétrica e aprendizado de máquina causal.** Only after identification, study propensity-score adjustment, inverse weighting, doubly robust estimating equations, influence functions, orthogonal scores and nuisance-learning methods. Bang–Robins (2005) formalizes a two-model route to consistency under its stated conditions; it is not a universal protection against all model misspecification. Chernozhukov et al. (2018) use orthogonal moments/cross-fitting and require specified nuisance convergence and sampling assumptions. Ordinary i.i.d. cross-fitting is not automatically justified for spatially or longitudinally dependent observations.
3. **Spatial and longitudinal causal inference / Inferência causal espacial e longitudinal.** Treat time-dependent confounding (marginal structural models), policy or environmental interventions across staggered periods (modern DiD), spatial confounding and location-to-location interference/spillovers. These mechanisms challenge assumptions used by conventional independent-unit causal inference. Interference must be represented by a meaningful treatment/exposure mapping; ordinary SUTVA is not assumed without justification. A satellite/SAR-derived environmental outcome or a spatial/Bayesian hierarchical covariance fit can aid measurement/estimation but does **not** by itself identify the causal effect of an environmental policy.

The third pathway is a **methodologically justified link** to the user's existing statistics of dependent data and SAR/optical imagery, and possible future environmental studies—not a claim that existing SAR research established causal identification. Spatial dependence, spatial confounding and spatial interference are distinct problems. Granger-type temporal predictability does not automatically imply potential-outcome causation. A GEE/marginal regression coefficient is not automatically a causal effect; a marginal structural model requires its own identification and weighting arguments.

The three original works not shown in this short list—Rubin (1974), Angrist–Imbens–Rubin (1996), and Athey–Imbens (2016)—remain in the underlying longer bibliography. All existing source entries, preparatory notes and crosslinks remain available there. Older Rubin (1974), Angrist–Imbens–Rubin (1996) and Athey–Imbens (2016) remain available in the source bibliography. The cross-guide links go to `#spatial-models`, `#theory`, `#regression` and `#sar` with no new claims of personal publications.

## Technical change and verification boundary

`assets/causal-library.js` is the last editorial layer on all ten HTML entry points and in prerender/Playwright test loaders. It changes only objects with stable ID `causal`. The guide is rendered by an isolated compact section in `assets/app.js`; CSS is scoped to `.causal-guide`. Previous research-card order, six-guide count, approved publication metadata, research filters and prior guide pathways are preserved.

The tests cover: three causal routes, nine visible/12 underlying citations, title/ID/DOI integrity, working bilingual links, four cross-guide anchors, 45 visible public readings in total, desktop layout, 390px and 320px mobile without horizontal overflow, and existing academic/publication regression tests.

**Verification limits:** source metadata, author/publisher abstracts and earlier curated records were checked; this is **not** independent reproduction of every book or paper theorem. In particular, the guide does not claim novel spatial causal estimators for the author's conditional ARMA models, intervention-effect estimates from SAR images, access to clinical data or a completed causal-research publication. Passing automated tests cannot prove scientific identification.
