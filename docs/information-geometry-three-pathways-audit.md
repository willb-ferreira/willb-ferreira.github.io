# Information Geometry — three research pathways (2026-09-29)

## Scope and decision

Only the **Information Geometry** research card and its public reading guide were reorganized. The guide keeps the stable `geometry` anchor, defaults to English, and has complete Brazilian Portuguese counterparts. The six-area site structure, the personal publications and their approved metadata, the academic records, the other five guides and the preceding long-form source records are unchanged.

The previous public guide contained five selected entries with a useful geodesic-test focus but offered only a two-step reading path. The new introductory format follows the other guides: common mathematical foundations, **three short routes**, eight linked starting references and three contextual cross-links. No unpublished geodesic estimator, test, proof, software result or private manuscript is disclosed.

The three linked routes are:

1. **Statistical manifolds and dual geometry / Variedades estatísticas e geometria dual** — Fisher–Rao information metrics, statistical manifolds, divergences, dual affine connections, and local versus global geometry.
2. **Geometric estimation and hypothesis testing / Estimação geométrica e testes de hipóteses** — divergence- or geodesic-based procedures, including published geodesic tests by Menéndez, Morales, Pardo and Salicrú; model-specific null calibration, identifiability and regularity. Pardo's 2006 divergence-inference book remains visible in Topic 2 rather than repeated here.
3. **Geometric computation and structured statistical models / Computação geométrica e modelos estatísticos estruturados** — Amari's natural gradient, numerical Fisher–Rao distances for multivariate normal distributions, and a carefully delimited bridge to PolSAR Wishart-classification methods based on stochastic distances.

**Essential distinctions:** A divergence is not generally a metric and need not equal an information-geodesic distance. A local quadratic divergence can induce a metric under suitable assumptions, but numerical integration and global geodesics require additional work. Natural gradient solves an optimization question; it does not by itself yield a calibrated test. The stochastic Wishart distances in the PolSAR work are **not represented as proven Fisher–Rao geodesics**. The published work of another author is not attributed as the website owner's research contribution.
