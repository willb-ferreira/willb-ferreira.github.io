## Correction and scientific distinction

The original guide selected Burbea & del Castillo (1992) as the geodesic-testing example but omitted the **specific article identified by the author**: M. L. Menéndez, D. Morales, L. Pardo and M. Salicrú, “Statistical tests based on geodesic distances” (1995). The latter is now the principal selected **testing** article. Burbea & del Castillo stays as a complementary study of Fisher-metric geodesic submanifolds. The 1997 article by the same four authors gives the next reading on `(h,Φ)`-entropy-induced metrics and an illustrative Pareto-family test. The original Amari and Amari–Nagaoka mathematical introductions stay first.

**Mathematical boundaries:** A divergence, a Riemannian metric derived from its local expansion and its global geodesic distance are distinct mathematical objects. A metric induced by `(h,Φ)`-entropy need not be identical to the Fisher–Rao metric. A test based on estimated geodesic distance needs the model's regularity, parameter identifiability, computability of the distance and a justified null distribution; no automatic universal χ² claim is made. These are published avenues of inference, not assertions of novel results by the site author.

## Five visible readings, in study order

1. **Shun-ichi Amari (2016),** *Information Geometry and Its Applications*. Introductory geometric foundations. Existing bibliographic link and metadata retained.
2. **Shun-ichi Amari and Hiroshi Nagaoka (2000),** *Methods of Information Geometry*. Differential-geometric foundations and dual connections. Existing bibliographic link and metadata retained.
3. **Jacob Burbea and Joan del Castillo (1992),** “Geodesic submanifolds of statistical models with location parameters.” *Computational Statistics & Data Analysis* **14**(3), 301–313. DOI: [10.1016/0167-9473(92)90041-D](https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-9473(92)90041-D). [Publisher abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/016794739290041D) checked for its geodesic-submanifold criteria and stated connection to geodesic tests. Existing record retained.
4. **María Luisa Menéndez, Domingo Morales, Leandro Pardo and Miquel Salicrú (1995),** “Statistical tests based on geodesic distances.” *Applied Mathematics Letters* **8**(1), 65–69. DOI: [10.1016/0893-9659(94)00112-P](https://doi.org/10.1016/0893-9659(94)00112-P). **New central reference.** Authors, title, publication year, journal, volume, issue and pages cross-checked in the [Universidad Complutense de Madrid publication index](https://produccioncientifica.ucm.es/investigadores/141997/colaboracion/investigador/6433?lang=de); DOI and author order cross-checked in [subsequent journal citations](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7516531/). The article's author-shared abstract, reproduced on researchers' public profiles, states that a geodesic-distance hypothesis-testing procedure and asymptotic test-statistic laws are proposed. **The publisher's original full text was not independently inspected; no detailed theorem or universal reference distribution is asserted.**
5. **María Luisa Menéndez, Domingo Morales, Leandro Pardo and Miquel Salicrú (1997),** “(h, Φ)-entropy differential metric.” *Applications of Mathematics* **42**(2), 81–98. DOI: [10.1023/A:1022214326758](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1022214326758). **New follow-up reference.** [Mathematical library primary catalogue and abstract](https://www.dml.cz/handle/10338.dmlcz/134347?show=full) checked: a Riemannian metric induced by (h,Φ)-entropy, calculated geodesic distances for selected models, a Pareto-family testing illustration and asymptotic results for estimated information matrices. Its full PDF is available there but its complete derivations were **not** independently reproduced during this editorial review.

**Not shown as a sixth geometry reading:** Leandro Pardo's 2006 book *Statistical Inference Based on Divergence Measures* remains in the underlying geometry bibliography and visible under Topic 2, where its primary pedagogical role is divergence-based inference. This avoids repeating the same visible item in adjacent short guides while keeping a cross-link.
