A starting point for each research area

Seven reading paths with foundations, questions to explore and publisher-linked references. These are curated starting points, not systematic reviews or advertised supervision positions.

How to use these guides. Start with an accessible reference, move to the foundations and read seminal articles with their assumptions in view. The paths are study suggestions, not rankings of the literature or a list of available supervision projects.

Spatial and spatio-temporal statistics

How can spatial dependence be represented without imposing an incoherent joint model?

Begin with the distinction between geostatistical data, lattice data, and point processes. For conditional spatial ARMA models, study how local specifications relate to valid joint distributions and what dependence implies for estimation.

Recommended background: Probability, linear algebra, random vectors; useful: likelihood and basic time-series models.

A possible route

  1. Start with Cressie to identify the type of spatial data and the covariance questions.
  2. Read Besag carefully when moving to conditional models on lattices.
  3. Use Diggle and Ribeiro to connect assumptions, inference and spatial prediction.

Selected reading

  1. Foundation1993

    Statistics for Spatial Data

    Noel A. C. Cressie

    A broad foundation: covariance, kriging, lattice models and point patterns.

  2. Seminal paper1974

    Spatial Interaction and the Statistical Analysis of Lattice Systems

    Julian Besag

    A foundational treatment of conditional specifications and spatial interactions on lattices.

  3. Next step2007

    Model-based Geostatistics

    Peter J. Diggle; Paulo J. Ribeiro Jr.

    A model-based route from spatial data to inference and prediction.

Statistical inference and asymptotic theory

What assumptions make an estimator or a test reliable, especially under dependence?

This area asks how procedures behave beyond a particular dataset: identifiability, bias, consistency, limiting distributions, efficiency, and information. The core habit is to state assumptions before interpreting an asymptotic result.

Recommended background: Mathematical statistics, probability, multivariable calculus and some real analysis.

A possible route

  1. Review classical estimation and risk with Lehmann and Casella.
  2. Work through consistency, M/Z-estimation and local asymptotic normality in van der Vaart.
  3. Use Godambe to connect estimating functions and optimality questions.

Selected reading

  1. Foundation1998

    Theory of Point Estimation, 2nd ed.

    E. L. Lehmann; George Casella

    A rigorous basis for estimation, optimality and decision-theoretic criteria.

  2. Next step1998

    Asymptotic Statistics

    A. W. van der Vaart

    The mathematical bridge to M-estimation, efficiency and empirical processes.

  3. Seminal paper1960

    An Optimum Property of Regular Maximum Likelihood Estimation

    V. P. Godambe

    A short early reference for the optimality perspective behind estimating functions.

Statistical image processing

What statistical structure is created by coherent imaging, speckle and polarimetric measurements?

The point is not merely image classification: it is to understand how acquisition, speckle, spatial dependence and the choice of observable determine an appropriate statistical model. Read physical foundations before choosing a probability law.

Recommended background: Probability distributions and statistical inference; helpful: complex-valued random vectors and linear algebra.

A possible route

  1. Start with Oliver and Quegan for the imaging chain and statistical observables.
  2. Read Goodman to understand the physical and statistical origins of speckle.
  3. Move to Lee and Pottier for polarimetric measurements, then examine applied modeling.

Selected reading

  1. Start here2004

    Understanding Synthetic Aperture Radar Images

    Chris Oliver; Shaun Quegan

    A practical and statistical introduction to SAR formation, noise and image analysis.

  2. Seminal paper1976

    Some fundamental properties of speckle

    J. W. Goodman

    The physical/statistical foundation for speckle contrast and multilook averaging.

  3. Next step2009

    Polarimetric Radar Imaging: From Basics to Applications

    Jong-Sen Lee; Eric Pottier

    Develops the polarimetric representation and relevant processing techniques.

  4. Research example2026

    A GAMLSS Framework for Crop Discrimination Using Geodesic Polarimetric SAR Parameters

    Willams B. F. da Silva; Avik Bhattacharya; Abraão D. C. Nascimento; Alejandro C. Frery

    An application-oriented connection to the research conducted in this group.

Regression and generalized estimating equations

How should regression account for non-Gaussian outcomes and correlated observations?

Work from the mean–variance relationship in generalized linear models toward estimating equations for correlated data. Distinguish modeling the marginal mean from specifying an entire joint distribution.

Recommended background: Linear models, likelihood, matrix calculus; useful: repeated measures and asymptotic inference.

A possible route

  1. Study link functions, variance functions and likelihood in McCullagh and Nelder.
  2. Read Liang and Zeger to understand working correlation and robust variance.
  3. Return to estimating-function optimality when choosing or comparing procedures.

Selected reading

  1. Foundation1989

    Generalized Linear Models, 2nd ed.

    P. McCullagh; J. A. Nelder

    The core reference for the generalized linear model framework.

  2. Seminal paper1986

    Longitudinal data analysis using generalized linear models

    Kung-Yee Liang; Scott L. Zeger

    Introduces generalized estimating equations for correlated responses.

  3. Start here2020

    Regression and Other Stories

    Andrew Gelman; Jennifer Hill; Aki Vehtari

    A readable complement on model interpretation, uncertainty and real-data decisions.

Time series and stochastic processes

How do temporal dependence, stationarity and model diagnostics interact?

Begin with covariance and the ARMA construction; then study identification, residual checks and forecast uncertainty. Non-Gaussian extensions connect this area directly to amplitude and intensity modeling for radar data.

Recommended background: Probability, linear algebra, regression and elementary stochastic processes.

A possible route

  1. Learn the core autocovariance and ARMA ideas in Brockwell and Davis.
  2. Use Box and colleagues for model-building, identification and diagnostics.
  3. Study a non-Gaussian SAR process as one methodological extension, not as a general time-series template.

Selected reading

  1. Start here2016

    Introduction to Time Series and Forecasting, 3rd ed.

    Peter J. Brockwell; Richard A. Davis

    An accessible introduction to stochastic time-series modeling and forecasting.

  2. Foundation2015

    Time Series Analysis: Forecasting and Control, 5th ed.

    George E. P. Box; Gwilym M. Jenkins; Gregory C. Reinsel; Greta M. Ljung

    Classical ARIMA identification, fitting and diagnostic workflow.

  3. Research example2023

    Generalized gamma ARMA process for synthetic aperture radar amplitude and intensity data

    Willams B. F. da Silva; Pedro M. Almeida-Junior; Abraão D. C. Nascimento

    An example of non-Gaussian ARMA modeling tied to SAR observables.

Information geometry

What can the geometry of probability models reveal about inference and optimization?

Information geometry studies families of distributions as geometric objects. Begin with Fisher information as a metric, then examine dual connections and divergence functions, separating geometric insight from application-specific claims.

Recommended background: Multivariable calculus, probability, mathematical statistics; helpful: differential geometry.

A possible route

  1. Begin with the opening chapters of Amari (2016) for the main objects and intuition.
  2. Continue with Amari and Nagaoka for the mathematical structure of dual connections.
  3. Use proper scoring rules as one bridge to divergences and statistical decisions.

Selected reading

  1. Start here2016

    Information Geometry and Its Applications

    Shun-ichi Amari

    A structured introduction with statistical and computational applications.

  2. Foundation2000

    Methods of Information Geometry

    Shun-ichi Amari; Hiroshi Nagaoka

    Advanced treatment of the geometry of statistical models and dual connections.

  3. Next step2007

    Strictly Proper Scoring Rules, Prediction, and Estimation

    Tilmann Gneiting; Adrian E. Raftery

    A complementary bridge between convexity, divergence and predictive evaluation.

07 / 07 · Research library

Statistical computing

How can a computational result be made reliable, reproducible and reusable?

Statistical computing is more than running software: simulation design, numerical error, reproducibility and interpretable implementation all affect the strength of a scientific claim. Start with an auditable workflow and deepen the Monte Carlo theory.

Recommended background: Basic programming, probability and introductory statistical inference.

A possible route

  1. Start with an R project that can be run from a clean environment.
  2. Use Peng and Wickham to structure data and document the workflow.
  3. Move to Robert and Casella for principled simulation and Monte Carlo diagnostics.

Selected reading

  1. Start here2023

    R for Data Science, 2nd ed.

    Hadley Wickham; Mine Çetinkaya-Rundel; Garrett Grolemund

    Free online introduction to practical, reproducible R workflows.

  2. Foundation2011

    Reproducible Research in Computational Science

    Roger D. Peng

    A concise argument for sharing the components needed to evaluate computation.

  3. Next step2014

    Tidy Data

    Hadley Wickham

    A useful organizing principle for data pipelines and reusable analysis.

  4. Advanced2004

    Monte Carlo Statistical Methods, 2nd ed.

    Christian P. Robert; George Casella

    A rigorous reference for Monte Carlo simulation and its statistical properties.
